Isaiah Oloyede is a technical product manager working at the intersection of energy and software. At National Grid, he works within the IT & Digital organization supporting the US Electric business, where the challenge of modernizing one of the world's most complex machines — the electric grid — is a daily reality rather than an abstraction.

His focus is the grid edge: distributed energy resources, distribution system operations, and distributed energy resource management system (DERMS) that will let utilities orchestrate millions of solar panels, batteries, and Electric Vehicles instead of a few hundred power plants. He believes the energy transition is as much a software, data, and market-design problem as a hardware one, and that the people best positioned to solve it are those fluent in both the physics of power systems and the craft of building products.

Isaiah holds a Master of Science in Nuclear Science and Engineering from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, a Master of Engineering in Electrical Engineering from Cornell University, and a Bachelor of Engineering with First Class Honors in Electrical and Electronic Engineering from the Federal University of Technology, Akure, Nigeria.

His academic training shapes how he learns. Isaiah is a deliberate, first-principles learner who builds structured curricula for himself — working through power systems engineering, distribution system modeling, and agentic AI with the rigor of a graduate program, mastery checkpoints included. This site is where he learns in public: a growing garden of notes on mathematics, engineering foundations, programming, and the evolving architecture of the modern grid.

He is based in Massachusetts.
