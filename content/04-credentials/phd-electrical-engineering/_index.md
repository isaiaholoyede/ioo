# PhD — Electrical Engineering

A PhD in Electrical Engineering is a research degree — the goal is not to learn more of what is already known, but to generate new knowledge. These notes document the advanced coursework, research methods, literature, and in-progress work that comprise the doctoral journey.

---

## Components of a PhD Program

### Advanced Coursework
Graduate-level courses beyond undergraduate EE. Typical areas:
- Advanced Power Systems Analysis
- Optimization Methods for Engineering
- Machine Learning for Power Systems
- Power Electronics and Control
- Stochastic Systems and Estimation
- Numerical Methods for Engineers

### Research Methodology
Formulating research questions, literature review processes, experimental design, simulation methodology, writing and publishing.

### Literature Review
Systematic review of existing research. Key journals: IEEE Transactions on Power Systems, IEEE Transactions on Power Delivery, IEEE Transactions on Smart Grid, Applied Energy.

### Research Tools
MATLAB/Simulink, Python (NumPy, SciPy, Pandas, PyTorch), OpenDSS, PowerWorld, PSS/E, PSCAD. LaTeX for writing.

### Thesis / Dissertation
Original research contribution. Proposal, defense, chapters, committee review. Timeline and milestone management.

### Teaching & Mentorship
TA responsibilities, office hours, mentoring junior students. Communication of complex technical material.

## Potential Research Areas

Given the broader context of this vault, likely research focus areas include:

- Distribution system optimization under high DER penetration
- Grid-forming inverter control for resilience
- Machine learning for distribution system state estimation
- Optimal DER dispatch in distribution networks
- Power system resilience metrics and optimization
- Cybersecurity for distribution automation systems
